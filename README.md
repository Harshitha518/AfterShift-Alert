# AfterShift Alert - 2026 Swift Student Challenge Winner
AfterShift Alert is my winning submission for Apple’s Swift Student Challenge 2026. It's an interactive Swift app playground designed to help night shift workers better understand how alert they are after work and make safer decisions before driving home. Night shift workers are usually forced to drive home after long hours of work during times when their body is naturally less alert due to cricadian rythm disruption and sleep deprivation. At these times, their reaction times are slow, attentions drops, and the risk of drowsy driving is singificantly higher, including the likely possibility of microsleeps and impaired judgement while driving. This makes the drive home after work very risky, especially as most tend to overestimate their alertness.

Addressing this issue, AfterShift Alert estimates altertness using a mathematical model which combines two major concepts, Process S (sleep pessure) and Process C (circadian rythm). The app playground calculates an alertness score (0 - 100) using a worker’s sleep history, hours awake, shift timing, and recent driving history. Sleep recovery is modeled using a nonlinear logistic function, where recovery improves with sleep but begins to plateau and is reduced by cumulative sleep debt over multiple days. Circadian rhythm is modeled using a 24 hour sinusoidal function with a trough around 4 am to represent natural biological lows in alertness. The model also includes wake duration penalties after extended time awake, dynamic weighting between sleep pressure and circadian rhythm depending on sleep deprivation, and biological score caps to prevent unrealistic scores under severe sleep loss.

To improve accuracy, users can optionally complete short cognitve and psychomotor tests, each measuring specific metrics like reaction time, attention, working memory, cognitive control, and mental speed. These performance measures refine the alertness score, making it more accurate than if it were entirely self reported.

After estimating alertness, AfterShift Alert includes an interactive simulator that allows users to explore different short term interventions like delaying departure by various times, taking various lengths of naps, various amount of caffeine intake, and smaller factors like light exposure. The projected alertness score is then proportionately updated based on the combination of selected interventions, helping workers understand how practical changes may reduce immediate driving risk (improve alertness) and empowering workers to make a quick decision to take any of these interventions.

Overall, AfterShift Alert is a decision support tool that estimates alertness and shows how different interventions would affect it, helping workers decide which steps are worth taking before they drive home.
